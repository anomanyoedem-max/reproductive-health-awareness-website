Understand the difference

PCOS / PMOS and Endometriosis

These are different reproductive health conditions. Some effects may overlap, but their main characteristics and clinical assessments differ.

TopicPCOS / PMOSEndometriosis
Main characteristicsAssociated primarily with hormonal, metabolic, and reproductive changes.Endometrial-like tissue occurs outside the uterus and may cause inflammation and adhesions.
Menstrual symptomsMay be associated with irregular or absent periods and ovulation.Often associated with painful periods and pelvic pain.
PainPain is not described as the defining feature.Pain is a major concern and may occur during menstruation or at other times.
FertilityOvulation changes may make conception more difficult for some people.May be associated with fertility difficulties, but does not automatically mean infertility.
AssessmentMay involve symptoms, menstrual patterns, hormonal features, and investigations.May involve symptom history, examination, ultrasound or MRI; laparoscopy may be considered in selected cases.
ManagementTailored to hormonal, metabolic, reproductive, and other health needs.May include pain relief, hormonal treatment, or surgery depending on the individual situation.

What they have in common

Both can affect menstrual health, fertility, reproductive health, quality of life, and physical or emotional well-being.

Why the difference matters

Similar symptoms can have different causes. Appropriate clinical assessment helps guide suitable care.

Reminder: PCOS / PMOS and endometriosis are not the same condition. This comparison cannot diagnose either condition.
