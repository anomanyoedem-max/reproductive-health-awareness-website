Observe patterns, not just single symptoms

Symptom Awareness

Noticing changes in your menstrual cycle and how symptoms affect everyday life can help you communicate clearly with a healthcare professional.

Recognize changes

Notice persistent changes in menstrual patterns, severe menstrual pain, recurring pelvic pain, or symptoms linked to particular points in the cycle.

Notice the impact

Record whether symptoms affect work, school, sleep, movement, relationships, or everyday activities.

Look for cyclical patterns

Some endometriosis-related symptoms may become worse around menstruation or recur at similar times in the cycle.

Seek professional assessment

Arrange a healthcare consultation for symptoms that are persistent, severe, recurrent, or concerning.

A simple symptom diary

Copy this checklist into a notebook or phone. It is not a diagnostic test; it helps organize information for a consultation.

  • Date and approximate time
  • Symptom type and location
  • Severity and duration
  • Whether menstruation is occurring or expected
  • How often the symptom happens
  • Possible effects on daily activities
  • Questions you want to ask a healthcare professional
Important reminder: Symptom awareness does not replace medical diagnosis.
