Pelvic and menstrual health

Endometriosis

A chronic condition in which tissue similar to the lining of the uterus is found outside the uterus. It can cause inflammation, pain, and adhesions.

What is endometriosis?

The supplied write-up explains that symptoms may change across the menstrual cycle and may become worse shortly before or during menstruation. Some people experience severe pelvic pain, while others have few or no noticeable symptoms.

Common symptoms

  • Painful menstrual periods
  • Pelvic pain outside menstruation
  • Pain during or after sexual intercourse
  • Painful urination or bowel movements
  • Blood in urine or rectal bleeding
  • Fatigue
  • Difficulty becoming pregnant
  • Other symptoms that repeatedly follow the menstrual cycle

Possible causes

The exact cause remains unknown. The write-up discusses proposed explanations including retrograde menstruation, estrogen, and possible genetic or immune-system factors; none is presented as a complete explanation.

Diagnosis

A healthcare professional may assess medical history and symptoms, perform a clinical examination, and use ultrasound or MRI. Laparoscopy may be considered in selected circumstances. Imaging may not detect every lesion, and symptoms alone cannot confirm the condition.

Types described in the write-up

  • Superficial peritoneal endometriosis: lesions on the peritoneum.
  • Ovarian endometriosis: endometriosis affecting the ovaries, sometimes forming an endometrioma.
  • Deep endometriosis: disease extending deeply into tissue or involving pelvic organs.

Treatment and management

Options may include pain-relieving medication, hormonal treatment, and surgery. The appropriate approach depends on symptoms, fertility plans, age, preferences, and clinical advice.

Endometriosis and fertility

Having endometriosis does not automatically mean a person is infertile. Some people conceive spontaneously, while others may need fertility support. Pregnancy should not be considered a cure or treatment for endometriosis.

When to seek care: Discuss persistent or severe menstrual or pelvic pain, painful urination or bowel movements, cyclical symptoms, fatigue, or fertility difficulties with a qualified healthcare professional.
