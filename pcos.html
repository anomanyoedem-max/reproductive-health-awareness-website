Hormonal and metabolic health

PCOS / PMOS

Learn about the condition described in the supplied write-up as Polyendocrine Metabolic Ovarian Syndrome (PMOS), previously known as Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS).

What is PCOS?

The supplied write-up describes PCOS as a common, long-term hormonal condition that can affect menstrual cycles, ovulation, fertility, metabolism, skin, hair, and mental health. Symptoms and severity vary from person to person.

Common symptoms and features

  • Irregular or absent menstrual periods
  • Irregular or absent ovulation
  • Difficulty becoming pregnant
  • Increased facial or body hair
  • Acne or oily skin
  • Hair thinning or hair loss
  • Weight-related or metabolic changes

Associated health concerns

The write-up notes associations with insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, abnormal cholesterol or blood-fat levels, cardiovascular disease, and mental-health concerns.

How is it assessed?

Assessment may include symptoms, medical history, and appropriate clinical investigations. The supplied material describes adult diagnosis as generally involving at least two of three features after other possible causes have been excluded: irregular or absent ovulation; evidence of increased androgen activity; and polycystic ovarian morphology on appropriate imaging. Ovarian appearance alone does not establish a diagnosis.

Management

There is no single approach that suits everyone. Management may address menstrual cycles, androgen-related symptoms, metabolic health, fertility goals, mental health, and longer-term health risks. Treatment decisions should be made with a qualified healthcare professional.

Terminology note: This page follows the terminology in the supplied write-up. Check current guidance from recognized medical organizations before public clinical use.
